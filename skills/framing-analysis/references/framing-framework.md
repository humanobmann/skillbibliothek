# Framing Framework

## 1. Frame model

Use Entman's four functions as the minimum analytical unit:

1. Problemdefinition: What is presented as the problem?
2. Kausalattribution: Who or what supposedly caused it?
3. Moralische Bewertung: Who is legitimate, blameworthy, betrayed, threatened, or deserving?
4. Handlungsempfehlung: What response is presented as necessary?

Do not reduce framing to isolated words. Treat words, images, metaphors, actor roles, temporal structure, and proposed remedies as a connected causal model.

## 2. Populist architecture

A robust populist pattern usually contains a moralized vertical conflict:

`legitimate people` versus `corrupt or illegitimate elite`

Populist radical-right communication often adds a horizontal boundary:

`national or native in-group` versus `non-native, culturally foreign, or otherwise excluded out-group`

Do not diagnose populism from simple language, emotion, provocation, anti-government criticism, or social-media use alone.

## 3. Narrative sequence

Check for this recurring structure:

`valued past -> loss -> blame -> crisis or revelation -> restoration`

Common claimed losses include:

- control
- security
- sovereignty
- freedom
- prosperity
- status
- cultural continuity
- institutional trust
- normality

## 4. Political roles

Code separately:

- hero
- victim
- villain
- traitor
- rescuer
- external enemy
- internal enemy

A message becomes especially identity-laden when ordinary disagreement is reframed as betrayal or illegitimate membership in the political community.

## 5. Emotional mechanisms

Do not merge all negative emotions.

- fear: uncertainty and difficult-to-control threat
- anger: identifiable blame and perceived injustice
- disgust: contamination or moral boundary violation
- nostalgia: idealized collective past and perceived loss
- humiliation or status loss: relative position of group
- pride: positive group identity
- triumph: victory and status confirmation

Treat emotional effects as conditional, not automatic.

## 6. Linguistic microstructure

Look for:

- short coordinated clauses
- repetition
- triads
- absolute quantifiers
- superlatives
- binary contrasts
- pronoun polarization
- rhetorical questions
- ridicule
- nicknames
- hyperbole
- strategic ambiguity
- euphemisms and neologisms
- screenshot or meme portability

No single feature is diagnostic by itself.

## 7. Media and platform layer

Separate four outcomes:

1. Reach: more people encounter the message.
2. Agenda: the topic becomes more salient.
3. Frame adoption: the interpretation is accepted or contested.
4. Evaluation: attitudes toward the actor change.

Never infer persuasion from engagement alone.

## 8. Common restoration frames

Examples of abstract frame families:

| Frame | Core loss | Typical culprit | Restoration promise |
|---|---|---|---|
| people versus elite | representation | political or cultural elite | restore popular control |
| loss of control | state capacity | government, borders, bureaucracy | take control back |
| national belonging | identity or continuity | migration, globalization, cultural change | national priority |
| freedom | autonomy | state, experts, supranational rules | remove restrictions |
| security and order | safety | crime, weak authority, uncontrolled migration | stronger control and sanctions |
| sovereignty | decision power | external institutions | national decision power |
| normality | social stability | ideological or institutional change | restore normality |
| decline | national status | governing class | renewal or rebirth |
| truth versus media | epistemic trust | media, experts, institutions | direct communication |

These frames also appear outside the radical right. Diagnose combinations and structure, not isolated vocabulary.
