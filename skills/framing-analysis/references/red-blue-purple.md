# Red Blue Purple Team Method

## Red Team

Purpose: attack the initial interpretation, not the political actor.

Test at least these alternatives:

- substantive policy agreement rather than persuasion effect
- economic or social conditions rather than message design
- normal opposition rhetoric rather than populism
- platform incentives rather than ideology
- government versus opposition position
- national political culture or electoral system
- speaker-specific personality or branding
- self-selection of audiences
- agenda effects without persuasion
- correlation mistaken for causation

Reject strong claims when the evidence supports only association.

## Blue Team

Purpose: preserve what is empirically defensible.

For each surviving claim state:

- observation
- best supporting evidence
- boundary condition
- what the evidence does not prove

Examples of robust general propositions:

- moralized people-versus-elite framing is central to populism
- radical-right populism often adds exclusionary national or nativist boundaries
- repetition can increase familiarity and perceived truth
- corrections generally reduce false belief on average
- negative emotional appeals occur more frequently in many populist communications, but effects vary by context
- social media changes exposure and amplification, but exposure is not equivalent to durable persuasion

## Purple Team

Purpose: convert analysis into a resilient, factual response.

### Response sequence

1. Facts first
   - Lead with the verified fact or real-world consequence.

2. Reveal the causal shortcut
   - Show what the original frame leaves out, conflates, or assumes.

3. Reconstruct the causal model
   - Replace monocausal blame with the strongest supported explanation.

4. Reframe around a stable value
   - Examples: equal rules, accountability, practical security, functioning institutions, measurable outcomes, rights, responsibilities, proportionality.

5. Prebunk the recurring pattern
   - Briefly explain the technique: single incident turned into general trend, disagreement turned into betrayal, institutional complexity reduced to a hidden enemy, correlation presented as proof of causation.

6. Check amplification risk
   - Avoid repeating provocative labels more than necessary.
   - Avoid centering the opponent's vocabulary when a clearer factual description exists.

## Counterframing template

Use this internal template:

`Verified reality -> missing context -> frame mechanism -> evidence-based causal model -> alternative frame -> concise response`

Example abstractly:

Original frame:
`The state has lost control because elites care more about outsiders than citizens.`

Analytical reconstruction:
`loss-of-control frame + elite betrayal + horizontal outgroup boundary + restoration promise`

Counterframe architecture:
`Use measurable state capacity, policy results, legal responsibilities, and distributional facts. Address failures directly where verified. Reject unsupported group blame. Reframe competence as whether institutions solve concrete problems under equal rules.`

Do not turn this template into a voting recommendation.
