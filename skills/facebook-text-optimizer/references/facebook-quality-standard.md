# Full Facebook Quality Standard

Use this reference for maximum optimization, deep audits, political posts, or difficult rewrites.

## 1. Strategy

- Define the post's communication goal before editing.
- Identify what the reader should know, feel, think, or do.
- Optimize for one dominant core message.
- Identify the real audience rather than writing for "everyone".
- Make the practical relevance explicit.

## 2. Post type

Choose the structure that fits the job: information, opinion, clarification, political demand, personal, mobilization, or reaction. Do not force every topic into the same formula.

## 3. Opening

- The first visible lines must earn attention through relevance.
- Use a clear position, conflict, verified surprise, observation, personal perspective, useful question, or contradiction.
- Remove generic setup and delayed introductions.
- Do not use deceptive curiosity gaps.

## 4. Information hierarchy

- Put the most useful or decisive information early.
- Prefer reader logic over the chronology of the author's research process.
- Use one main job per paragraph.
- Ensure every paragraph advances the core message.

## 5. Dramaturgy

Useful patterns include:

- hook -> problem -> explanation -> evidence -> meaning -> position -> solution -> close
- claim -> proof -> meaning -> consequence
- problem -> cause -> solution
- situation -> conflict -> turn -> close
- question -> answer -> evidence -> context
- myth -> reality -> evidence
- person -> problem -> system -> solution

Choose the simplest pattern that fits the content.

## 6. Mobile readability

- Use compact paragraphs and visible whitespace.
- Avoid dense blocks and long chains of subordinate clauses.
- Make the strongest point visible early.
- Keep the post as long as necessary and no longer.
- Do not chase a universal ideal word count.

## 7. Language

Prefer:

- familiar words
- concrete nouns
- active verbs
- clear actors and actions
- direct syntax
- examples that make abstractions understandable

Avoid:

- nominal style
- bureaucratic filler
- unnecessary jargon
- artificial foreign words
- avoidable passive voice
- vague abstract language when a concrete consequence is available

## 8. Numbers and evidence

A number should explain, not merely impress. Add period, comparison, scale, trend, affected group, or practical meaning when needed.

Check every material number, date, name, office, quotation, institution, and causal statement.

Prefer primary and authoritative sources for important public claims.

A source must support the exact sentence, not merely the general topic.

## 9. Voice and rhythm

- Write naturally without becoming sloppy.
- Vary sentence length.
- Isolate a key sentence only when it deserves visual weight.
- Preserve a recognizable human voice.
- Respect explicit personal, organizational, or role-specific voice rules.

## 10. Emotion

Useful emotional registers include hope, solidarity, justified anger, empathy, joy, pride, surprise, concern, humor, and confidence.

Emotion must emerge from the subject and evidence. Do not use unsupported outrage as a substitute for substance.

Combine fact, human meaning, and value judgment where appropriate.

## 11. Storytelling

Use concrete situations to explain systems. A simple story can contain person or situation, goal, obstacle, consequence, and meaning.

Never present an invented person or anecdote as real. Mark hypotheticals clearly.

## 12. Rhetoric

Use devices selectively:

- rule of three
- repetition
- contrast
- rhetorical question
- antithesis
- escalation

Avoid stacked rhetorical questions, constant superlatives, and repetitive outrage.

Distinguish purposeful repetition from redundancy.

## 13. Credibility

Separate when useful:

- fact
- interpretation
- value judgment
- political demand

Do not state responsibility, causation, or motive more strongly than the evidence allows.

Make uncertainty visible rather than hiding it.

## 14. Counterarguments and red team

Identify the strongest reasonable counterargument. Decide whether it needs one concise answer.

For political or controversial posts, test:

- easiest sentence to disprove
- context-vulnerable number
- causation errors
- overgeneralization
- motive attribution
- unfair comparison
- one-sided evidence
- screenshot risk
- whether the core claim still survives

## 15. Originality

Do not merely restate a headline, press release, or third-party post. Add useful original value through analysis, explanation, regional relevance, firsthand perspective, verified context, a new connection, or consequence.

## 16. Platform hygiene

Avoid:

- clickbait
- engagement bait
- forced commenting prompts
- random tagging
- unrelated hashtag blocks
- spam-like capitalization

Use a call to action only when it serves the communication goal.

Use hashtags and mentions sparingly and purposefully.

Emojis are optional style devices, not a quality requirement.

## 17. Comment value

Invite genuine discussion through a real question, unresolved trade-off, practical experience, or meaningful contrast. Do not manufacture interaction.

## 18. Image and caption coordination

When a visual is part of the post:

- the visual should stop attention or carry the core claim
- the caption should explain, support, contextualize, and conclude
- visual and caption must not contradict each other

Consider alt text, captions, contrast, readable type, and non-color-only meaning when applicable.

## 19. Editing sequence

Use separate passes:

1. strategy and core message
2. structure and hierarchy
3. evidence and explanation
4. reader flow
5. style and rhythm
6. shortening
7. factual verification
8. copyediting
9. mobile preview
10. final quality gate

Read difficult passages aloud or simulate spoken cadence. If a sentence is hard to say naturally, simplify it.

Publication and learning

When the task includes publication strategy, judge success by the post's actual goal rather than likes alone. Possible metrics include meaningful comments, shares, clicks, profile visits, follows, participation, or the intended downstream action.

Treat comments as qualitative reader feedback. Repeated confusion often signals an explanation problem in the post.

Analyze both strong and weak posts by topic, hook, length, emotion, evidence, visual, ending, and comment quality. Do not automatically attribute weak performance to algorithmic suppression.

## 22. Common myths to reject

Do not assume:

- shorter is always better
- more hashtags always increase reach
- more comments guarantee distribution
- every post needs a comment prompt
- emotion is inherently unserious
- factual writing cannot contain a clear position
- a strong image can permanently rescue weak or misleading copy

## 23. Political content rules

For political posts, additionally require:

- no claim stronger than its evidence
- fact and opinion remain distinguishable
- opposing positions are represented accurately enough to answer fairly
- no unnecessary group generalization
- political responsibility is named only when supportable
- system criticism is not replaced by personal abuse
- policy consequences are translated into everyday meaning
- the strongest counterargument is considered
- sources are available for material claims
- a final adversarial fact check is passed

Aim for a post that remains defensible when every sentence is checked by a skeptical reader or journalist.

## 24. Final rule

A good Facebook post is not optimized along a single dimension. It should be:

- well researched
- clearly reasoned
- easy to understand
- human in voice
- structurally disciplined
- emotionally credible
- rhetorically controlled
- visually coherent when media is involved
- platform-appropriate without gimmicks
- improved through evidence and feedback over time

The goal is not to trick people into clicking. The goal is to give them a good reason to stop, understand, trust, remember, and respond.
