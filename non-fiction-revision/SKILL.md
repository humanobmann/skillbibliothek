# Non Fiction Revision

Treat revision as a sequence of levels. Fix higher-level problems before sentence polish.

## Revision order

1. Thesis
   - State the chapter or document's central claim in one sentence.
   - Remove sections that do not advance it or clearly support context.
2. Structure
   - Check whether sections build logically.
   - Move definitions, evidence and examples to the point where the reader needs them.
3. Evidence
   - Identify unsupported claims, weak examples and outdated evidence.
   - Hand factual verification to fact-check when available.
4. Explanation
   - Define specialist terms after explaining the underlying idea in plain language.
   - Prefer concrete examples before abstraction.
5. Reader flow
   - Remove repetition, unnecessary recap and abrupt conceptual jumps.
   - Keep paragraphs focused on one main job.
6. Style
   - Shorten sentences that carry multiple independent ideas.
   - Prefer direct verbs and concrete nouns.
   - Preserve the author's voice rather than flattening it into generic prose.

## Reader test

For each section ask:

- What should the reader understand here?
- What must they already know?
- Is the evidence strong enough for the claim?
- Is an example needed before the theory?
- Does the next section follow naturally?

## Book-series continuity

When revising a series, check terminology, recurring concepts, tone, chapter patterns, cross-references and factual assumptions against the series bible or canonical project files.

## Output

When the user wants revision rather than critique, return the revised text as the primary result. For large manuscripts, also summarize the material structural changes and unresolved evidence gaps.

## Quality gates

Do not finish until:

- the thesis is clear
- section order is defensible
- major repetition is removed
- difficult concepts are explained before they are labeled
- evidence gaps are identified
- voice remains consistent
