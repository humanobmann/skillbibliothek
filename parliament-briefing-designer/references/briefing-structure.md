# Briefing Structure

Use this model for a concise Austrian parliamentary working paper. Adapt only when the parliamentary use case requires it.

## Metadata

Include document type, topic, recipient or office, occasion, status, stand date and evidence basis. Do not infer a person's current function.

## 1. Zweck

State the concrete use case, such as committee preparation, written question, expert meeting, internal positioning or decision support. Keep it to three to five lines.

## 2. Kurzlage

Summarise the issue, current relevance, parliamentary problem and recommended direction in no more than ten lines.

## 3. Politische Hauptlinie

Give one source-consistent line that guides the briefing. Mark proposed wording as a proposal, not as an attributed position.

## 4. Zentrale Fakten

Use three to five decisive facts. For each include value or claim, period, scope, source status and caveat. Move supporting detail to an appendix.

## 5. Bewertung

Separate:

1. verified fact
2. political assessment
3. parliamentary conclusion

## 6. Line to Take

Provide a short speaking line supported by the evidence. Do not use quotation marks unless quoting a verified person.

## 7. Gegenargumente

Use a two-column table with the strongest plausible objection and a concise evidence-based response. Do not invent weak opponents.

## 8. Parlamentarische Hebel

List only procedurally plausible levers. Distinguish written questions, committee work, motions, budget control, legislative initiatives and public communication.

## 9. Konkrete Fragen

Group eight to twelve questions by theme. Each question must be answerable by the addressee and remain within parliamentary competence. Put longer catalogues in an appendix.

## 10. Ergebnis oder nächster Schritt

End with one concrete decision, request or parliamentary move, including owner and timing only when known.
